\# SAY-FER BIO - DESIGN SYSTEM



\## 1. Brand Direction



Say-Fer Bio should feel:



\- advanced

\- scientific

\- credible

\- future-facing

\- biological

\- engineered

\- sophisticated

\- minimal

\- premium

\- calm

\- confident



The brand exists at the intersection of:



BIOLOGY + ENGINEERING + FOOD TECHNOLOGY



The website should communicate that relationship visually.



It should not look like:



\- a generic agriculture company

\- a generic SaaS startup

\- a laboratory equipment catalogue

\- a science textbook

\- a cyberpunk biotechnology concept



The quality benchmark is premium biotechnology storytelling similar in ambition to websites such as Anomaly Bio.



References are for quality, motion, and art-direction targets only.



Do not visually clone them.



\## 2. Primary Visual Language



The entire homepage should be built around two primary visual elements:



SPINDLE

\+

CELL GROWTH



These elements should tell the story of controlled biological cultivation.



Avoid adding unrelated 3D objects simply to make the website look more complex.



\## 3. The Spindle



The main hero object is a stainless-steel cultivation spindle / agitator shaft.



Reference characteristics:



\- long central shaft

\- approximately three impeller assemblies

\- multiple metal blades

\- cylindrical mounting details

\- stainless-steel construction

\- realistic industrial proportions

\- smooth machined surfaces

\- subtle bevels

\- controlled metallic reflections



The spindle should feel:



\- engineered

\- precise

\- premium

\- physical

\- industrial

\- believable



It must NOT be surrounded by:



\- reactor tank

\- pipes

\- valves

\- cooling jacket

\- technical plumbing

\- industrial control equipment



The spindle alone is enough.



\## 4. Spindle Composition



On the landing experience, the spindle should be visually dominant.



Possible composition:



\- slightly off-centre

\- vertical or slightly angled

\- floating in a dark spatial environment

\- enough depth to clearly feel three-dimensional

\- strong controlled highlights on the stainless steel



The spindle should not look like a static product image pasted beside text.



It should feel integrated into the spatial composition.



\## 5. Spindle Motion



The spindle should rotate continuously at a calm, controlled speed.



Its movement should suggest:



\- precision

\- cultivation

\- engineering

\- controlled biological processing



Scroll progression may influence:



\- camera position

\- spindle scale

\- spindle orientation

\- framing

\- lighting

\- spatial position

\- section transitions



Do not make the rotation frantic.



Do not tie RPM directly to scroll-wheel velocity.



\## 6. Cell Visual System



Cells form the biological environment of the site.



They should resemble soft cell-like bubbles containing subtle nuclei.



Visual qualities:



\- organic

\- translucent

\- slightly irregular

\- soft membrane

\- visible but restrained nucleus

\- different sizes

\- different depths

\- subtle internal variation

\- slow floating motion

\- gentle biological pulsation where appropriate



They should NOT resemble:



\- cartoon bubbles

\- soap bubbles

\- generic particles

\- glowing sci-fi orbs

\- emojis

\- textbook cell diagrams



\## 7. Cell Proliferation Story



Cell density should evolve as the visitor moves through the homepage.



Suggested progression:



Hero:

very few cells



Innovation:

initial cells appear



Aim / Goal:

cell population increases



Technology:

visible cell proliferation becomes more obvious



Products / Services:

richer biological environment



Later sections:

high but controlled population



Final Contact:

visual intensity may gradually calm again



The proliferation should communicate biological growth without needing literal explanatory animation.



\## 8. Text-Safe Cell Behaviour



Cells must never materially compromise readability.



The page should define visual safe zones around important typography.



Use:



\- exclusion zones

\- opacity gradients

\- masks

\- spawn avoidance

\- lower density around text

\- depth fading

\- natural transparency



When a cell approaches a protected text region:



its opacity should gradually decrease.



When it leaves:



its opacity may gradually recover.



The effect must feel continuous and organic.



Do not make cells suddenly blink in and out.



\## 9. Hero



The hero should be minimal.



Primary ingredients:



\- Say-Fer Bio identity

\- concise headline

\- minimal supporting text

\- 3D spindle

\- sparse cell environment

\- premium dark spatial atmosphere



Do not overload the hero with:



\- large paragraphs

\- many buttons

\- feature cards

\- statistics

\- diagrams

\- unnecessary navigation elements



The 3D object should have room to breathe.



\## 10. Innovation Section



Purpose:



introduce the idea of Say-Fer's biological innovation.



Visual progression:



\- spindle remains part of the environment

\- cells become more noticeable

\- subtle spatial or camera transition



The visual should imply growth and controlled cultivation.



\## 11. Aim / Goal Section



Purpose:



communicate the broader company mission.



Keep draft copy provisional until the client provides final wording.



The cell environment may become more active here.



Avoid unverified environmental or sustainability claims.



\## 12. Technology Section



This should be one of the strongest storytelling moments.



The spindle may:



\- move closer

\- occupy a different composition

\- receive stronger controlled lighting

\- become more technically prominent



Cells continue proliferating around it.



Explain the technology conceptually rather than turning the section into an engineering schematic.



\## 13. Products Section



Products require clear information hierarchy.



Do not default to a generic grid of rounded cards.



Possible approaches:



\- editorial product rows

\- strong typography

\- product imagery

\- sectional transitions

\- restrained technical metadata



Final layout depends on actual product information.



\## 14. Services Section



Services should be clear and commercially understandable.



Avoid excessive visual decoration.



The 3D environment may remain present but should not reduce comprehension.



\## 15. Team Section

Possible design language:



\- publication-style hierarchy

Avoid a generic three-card blog layout if possible.



\## 18. Contact Section



The final CTA should be minimal and confident.



The cell environment may gradually reduce in density.



The spindle may move into a calm final position.



The ending should feel resolved rather than visually chaotic.

\## 20. Color Direction



Base environment:



\- near black

\- warm black

\- very dark charcoal



Primary foreground:



\- soft white

\- ivory

\- restrained light grey



Primary biological accent:



\- deep natural green derived from Say-Fer's existing logo



Secondary biological / technical accent:



\- restrained cyan or aqua derived from the logo



Spindle:



\- stainless steel

\- neutral metallic tones

\- controlled white highlights



Cells:



\- muted green

\- subtle cyan

\- translucent neutral membrane

\- soft nucleus differentiation



Do not use every color from the current Say-Fer logo simultaneously.



The website should feel significantly more restrained than the existing logo artwork.



\## 21. Lighting



Lighting should be cinematic but controlled.



Spindle lighting:



\- strong readable metallic edge highlights

\- subtle environmental reflection

\- controlled contrast

\- no excessive bloom



Cell lighting:



\- soft

\- biological

\- translucent

\- lower contrast than spindle



The spindle should remain the visual anchor.



\## 22. Typography



Typography should combine:



EDITORIAL AUTHORITY

\+

TECHNICAL PRECISION



Use:



\- strong editorial display typography for major statements

\- clean modern sans-serif for body copy

\- compact technical labels where useful



Avoid:



\- generic startup font hierarchy

\- excessive all-caps

\- novelty futuristic fonts

\- overly thin unreadable text



Major statements should have generous breathing room.



\## 23. Layout Principles



Favor:



\- generous negative space

\- asymmetric composition

\- deliberate imbalance

\- large visual moments

\- controlled alignment

\- minimal UI chrome

\- integrated 3D composition



Avoid:



\- repetitive card grids

\- boxed sections everywhere

\- excessive containers

\- excessive pills

\- filling every empty area



Empty space is part of the visual system.



\## 24. Motion Principles



Motion should feel expensive.



Use:



\- slow transitions

\- carefully timed easing

\- smooth scroll-linked progression

\- restrained text reveals

\- subtle camera movement

\- intentional object movement



Avoid:



\- excessive bounce

\- rapid zooms

\- chaotic parallax

\- unnecessary cursor-following effects

\- animation on every UI element

\- motion without narrative purpose



\## 25. Landing Page Text Density



The opening experience should contain limited text.



The goal is immediate visual impact.



Priority:



1\. brand

2\. concise positioning

3\. spindle

4\. spatial atmosphere

5\. subtle cell presence



Additional explanation comes later.



\## 26. Responsive Behaviour



Desktop:



\- complete visual experience

\- high-quality spindle rendering

\- richer cells

\- controlled lighting

\- smooth camera progression



Tablet:



\- preserve spindle

\- reduce cell density

\- simplify effects if necessary



Mobile:



\- preserve core identity

\- fewer cells

\- lower DPR where necessary

\- simpler lighting

\- reduced visual complexity

\- stronger text-safe regions

\- excellent readability



Do not simply disable everything on mobile.



\## 27. Performance Philosophy



Visual quality does not justify poor performance.



Prefer:



\- instancing

\- geometry reuse

\- reasonable polygon counts

\- efficient materials

\- adaptive cell density

\- sensible DPR

\- restrained post-processing



The website should remain smooth on ordinary modern phones and laptops.



\## 28. Existing Brand Logo



The existing Say-Fer Bio logo contains:



\- green biological imagery

\- plant/root motifs

\- chemistry references

\- cyan accents



For the website interface:



do not reproduce all of these motifs everywhere.



Use the existing logo where required, but develop a cleaner and more restrained surrounding visual system.



A future brand/logo refinement may be considered separately.



\## 29. Reference Direction

SPINDLE + CELLS + CULTIVATION

## 4. Homepage Spindle Motion

The old continuous autoplay rule is cancelled.

The spindle rotates only around its own longitudinal axis.

Motion is directly controlled by continuous scroll.

Required interaction:

scroll down slowly
-> slow forward axial rotation

scroll down quickly
-> faster forward axial rotation

scroll up slowly
-> slow reverse axial rotation

scroll up quickly
-> faster reverse axial rotation

stop scrolling
-> stop scroll-driven rotation

No autonomous rotation.

No independent momentum.

No section-based axial rotation pose system.

Minimal smoothing may remove jitter but must not make the spindle keep spinning after scroll stops.

## 5. Homepage Spindle Material

The V2 spindle must be production-quality.

Target:

- realistic stainless steel
- brushed / polished lab-grade metal
- proper bevels
- believable blade thickness
- physically plausible roughness
- controlled specular reflections
- clean machining
- subtle imperfection
- strong but restrained edge highlights

Avoid:

- flat grey
- plastic metal
- pure mirror chrome
- blown-out white blade faces
- primitive box geometry

Subtle reflections/highlight bands may be influenced by the left-side content environment.

Do NOT show literal readable mirrored copy in the metal.

The reflection should feel like vague environmental light/value contrast from the nearby page.

## 6. Homepage Cells

- restrained biological drift
- natural material response

Avoid:

- soap bubbles

- glowing sci-fi particles

Scroll down
-> progression forward

Scroll up
